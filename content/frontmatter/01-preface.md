
# Preface {.unnumbered}

In the Name of Allah, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful All praise is due to
Allah, Lord of the Worlds. May peace and blessings be upon Prophet Muhammad, his
family, and his Companions. 

A fatwa is more than a pronouncement of a legal ruling or an exposition of
juristic differences. It is a solemn scholarly trust, intended to guide the
questioner towards obedience to Allah, avoidance of His prohibitions, and the
realisation of the higher objectives of Islamic Law (Maqāṣid al-Sharīʿah). 

This book brings together selected fatwas originally issued through a programme
hosted by Islam Online between approximately 2003 and 2007. At the time, I was
teaching at the prestigious International Islamic University Malaysia (IIUM),
and had decided to devote part of my time each week to answering questions from
Muslims living predominantly outside the Muslim world, often described as Muslim
minorities, diaspora communities, or Muslim communities abroad. I recall with
genuine satisfaction the many late-night hours spent responding to their
inquiries, often necessitated by differences in time zones. It was a deeply
rewarding experience, inspired by a commitment to sharing knowledge and serving
those seeking religious guidance. Some readers subsequently encouraged me to
compile a selection of these responses for wider benefit, and this volume is the
fruit of that encouragement. 

In formulating these fatwas, I sought to reconcile sound juristic reasoning with
the particular circumstances of each questioner, taking due account of time,
place, prevailing customs, and practical consequences. I deliberately avoided
unnecessary juristic controversies and lengthy discussions, recognising that the
questioner’s primary concern was to understand the applicable religious ruling.
At the same time, I endeavoured to illuminate the underlying wisdom and
objectives of Islamic legal provisions, thereby facilitating a clearer
understanding of the Divine Law and its intended purposes. 

Underlying this approach is a fundamental conviction: the true purpose of a
fatwa is not merely to declare what is lawful or unlawful, but to guide and
reform the questioner, enabling faithful observance of Allah’s commandments and
abstention from His prohibitions. A fatwa should therefore facilitate religious
compliance without undue hardship, while preserving the integrity of Islamic
legal principles without unwarranted concessions. 

Nearly a quarter of a century has elapsed since these fatwas were issued. Some
of the circumstances surrounding them may have changed, while others remain
substantially the same. Readers are therefore invited to bear this historical
and contextual dimension in mind when considering the responses, which were
formulated in relation to particular questions and circumstances. 

These fatwas are sincere scholarly endeavours, not claims of infallibility. I
pray that Almighty Allah accepts this humble effort, makes it beneficial to its
readers, and forgives its shortcomings.
