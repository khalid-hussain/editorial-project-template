# Judicial Affairs

## Definition of _Fatwa_

\QUESTION{194}{1}{9}{Submitted by Mike on Feb 25, 2003}

I’m interested in the meaning of _fatwa_ and its amenability to being redefined.
Isn’t a _fatwa_ supposed to be the truth? If so, how can the truth be redefined?
I am upset with some groups, which---it seems to me---whenever they run into
recruiting problems, they issue a new _fatwa_ to help their cause.

\ANSWER

In the Name of Allah, Most Gracious, Most Merciful.
All praise and thanks are due to Allah, and peace and blessings be upon His Messenger.

_Fatwa_ means to give accurate information about the legal ruling regarding an
issue. A scholar who has achieved the requirements of issuing _fatwa_ should do
this exercise. This is because a mufti is signing off on behalf of Allah.
Therefore, he has to fear Allah about what he says. As for those who are
inventing new _fatwas_ without having the requirements, they will be punished on
the Day of Judgment.

Allah Almighty knows best.





## Differences between schools of fiqh

\QUESTION{195}{2}{9}{Submitted by Meeran on Jul 5, 2004}

What should be the stance of common Muslims regarding different schools of
thought?

\ANSWER

In the Name of Allah, Most Gracious, Most Merciful.
All praise and thanks are due to Allah, and peace and blessings be upon His
Messenger.

The schools of thought being the interpretations of the scholars of the meanings
of the Sharīʿah texts, one should have respect for all schools of thought, and
one should avoid insulting or undermining any school of thought as long as this
school is within the Islamic framework. For instance, the founders of the
schools were friends who accepted the opinions of one another. Furthermore, they
used to pray behind one another. They loved each other, and therefore the
followers should follow the path of the founders.

Allah Almighty knows best.




## Misconceptions about applying Sharīʿah law

\QUESTION{196}{3}{9}{Submitted by Kamal Funsho from Nigeria on Nov 28, 2005}

Respected scholar, _as-Salām ʿAlalykum_,

I think that the fear and concerns of many non-Muslim communities about applying
the Islamic laws (Sharīʿah) can be attributed to their misunderstanding of the
nature of the Islamic political system. They believe that an Islamic state is a
theocratic one that is supposed to be ruled and administered by clergymen and
that it rules out any participation of communities of other religions---if it
even accepts their existence from the very beginning. How can such a claim be
refuted?

\ANSWER

_Wa ʿalaykum as-Salām wa raḥmatullāhi wa barakātuh_.

In the Name of Allah, Most Gracious, Most Merciful.
All praise and thanks are due to Allah, and peace and blessings be upon His
Messenger.

Thank you for your question. Truly, Islam nowadays is deeply misunderstood by
many non-Muslims as well as by Muslims due to their improper understanding of
its noble and comprehensive teachings. For instance, many Muslims think that a
state can only be considered to practise Islam when it implements Islamic
criminal law, whereas they don’t give similar importance to the implementation
of other laws such as political, economic and social. This is a partial and very
unacceptable understanding of Islam. Certainly, it will never be successful;
rather, it will defame Islam and increase people’s misunderstanding of it. At
the same time, many non-Muslims think that Islam is only concerned about killing
or cutting off peoples’ hands and heads. They ignore that Islam is about
tolerance, transparency, social, economic and political justice, productivity,
advancement, efficiency, cooperation and comprehensive development for all
aspects of human life.

All in all, I believe that Muslims as well as non-Muslims should deepen their
understanding of the teachings of Islam. They should comprehensively learn more
and more about the essences, objectives, and purposes of Islam. It is only
through proper and correct understanding of Islam that all false accusations
would be refuted.





## Does Islam Call for Killing?

\QUESTION{197}{4}{9}{Submitted by Drame Mohamed from Angola on Jun 15, 2003}

Does Islam call for killing the other?

\ANSWER

In the Name of Allah, Most Gracious, Most Merciful.
All praise and thanks are due to Allah, and peace and blessings be upon His
Messenger.

Surely, Islam does not call for killing and taking the life of any innocent
human being. Killing an innocent person is regarded as a major sin. Accordingly,
Islam should not be associated with any killing in its name. The life of the
human being is sacred in Islam, and nobody has a right to take or destroy it.

Therefore, I appeal to you and all others to correct this wrong image about
Islam, which is about peace, love, compassion and peaceful co-existence with
others.

It is enough to know that the greeting in Islam stands for peace and safety of
all peoples, regardless of their religion. Islam calls for constructive
dialogue, intellectual discourse, and mutual respect among the members of
different faiths and religions. It also rejects all forms of violence, killing,
murdering, and injustice.

Let us know more about the true and real face of this great and wonderful
religion. Believe me; Islam came for the interest and prosperity of mankind on
this earth. May Allah guide all of us to the right path.

Allah Almighty knows best.




## Verbally denouncing one’s faith to save one’s life

\QUESTION{198}{5}{9}{Submitted by Nabil from Canada on Mar 31, 2003}

If a Muslim’s life was in danger, could he denounce Islam in order to save his
life, while in his heart he still accepts Islam?

\ANSWER

In the Name of Allah, Most Gracious, Most Merciful.
All praise and thanks are due to Allah, and peace and blessings be upon His
Messenger.

Yes, he/she is allowed to do this based on Allah’s statement, “Whoever
disbelieves in Allah after their belief---not those who are forced while their
hearts are firm in faith, but those who embrace disbelief wholeheartedly---they
will be condemned by Allah and suffer a tremendous punishment” (Al-Naḥl,
16:106).

Furthermore, refer to the incident of the family of Yāsir: he, his wife
Sumayyah, and their son, ʿAmmār. Yāsir and his wife were killed when they
refused to denounce Islam, while their son ʿAmmār was saved when he accepted to
denounce Islam. The Prophet \hon{\PBUH}{\BODY} said about them: “Be patient, O
family of Yāsir, Heaven is granted to you.” Regarding this incident, Allah
revealed the abovementioned verse of Sūrah Al-Naḥl.

Allah Almighty knows best.
