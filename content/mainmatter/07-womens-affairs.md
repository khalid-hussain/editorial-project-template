# Women's Affairs

## _Ḥijāb_ and Adornment

### The basic of _ḥijāb_

Submitted by Nayeefa from Japan on Jan 5, 2004

_As-Salāmu ʿalaykum_, Dear Br. Moustapha.

My question is about the ruling of ‘ḍarūrah’ or ‘exception to the rule out of
necessity’. How far and under what circumstances can it be applicable? For
example, would necessity make it permissible to have a lesser _ḥijāb_ (that is,
to cover the rest of the body but leave the head uncovered) in certain
non-Muslim countries? Also, would it make usury permissible for Muslims who are
living under certain conditions, etc.? What happens when scholars/muftis/sheikhs
are divided concerning certain issues as they apply _ijtihād_? Can’t the Muslim
Ummah agree on one single standard _fatwa_ regarding controversial issues?

I recently read a BBC news report concerning Sheikh Mohammed Sayed Tantawi’s
stance on banning of the _ḥijāb_ in France; he held that France has the right to
impose such a ban. I strongly disagree with his view. What are the Muslim
parents living in France supposed to do now? Obviously, not everyone can afford
to send their daughter to private schools. What happens to a widow who is the
breadwinner of the family and for her to work in the public sector would entail
her having to work bare-headed as France proceeds to ban _ḥijāb_ in the
workforce, too? What should the Muslims in France do?

\ANSWER

_Wa ʿalaykum as-Salām wa raḥmatullāhi wa barakātuh_.

In the Name of Allah, Most Gracious, Most Merciful.
All praise and thanks are due to Allah, and peace and blessings be upon His
Messenger.

Thank you very much for your question. Truly, it is very disheartening and
disturbing to see what is happening to Muslims all over the world nowadays.
Humiliation, oppression, and depression are becoming essential parts of Muslims’
life. However, it is even more disturbing when Muslim scholars and learners fail
to agree on hot issues pertaining to Muslims such as the issue of _ḥijāb_ in
France and elsewhere.

I deeply wish that France did not have a culture that would declare this war
against _ḥijāb_. France’s secularism has lost sight of its own fundamentals.
There is no doubt that banning _ḥijāb_ or any religious symbols in France is a
slap in the face to secularism, as it is a clear violation of secularism’s own
principles. One might deduce that this policy will mark the beginning of the
destruction of secularism in France by its own advocates and so-called
protectors.

Having said that, and taking into account the powerlessness of the Muslim Ummah,
I call upon all beloved mothers, sisters, and daughters to do their level best
to avoid any clash leading to more destruction and humiliation. It is hoped that
Muslims and advocates of freedom will prevent such a law from taking place in a
secular France. If free thinkers and protectors of secularism keep quiet on this
issue, they should anticipate that one day the same policy makers might decide
to declare war against freedom of religion, freedom of the press, and freedom of
many other things.

All things considered, I believe the status of many helpless Muslim women would
be considered necessity (_ḍarūrah_), meaning there is no sin upon them in
accepting such an unjust and undemocratic policy for the time being. They
shouldn’t endanger their future or the future of their kids. Their daughters
must go the universities; they shouldn’t stop their education at all. Workers
also should not quit their jobs. They should continue the legal fight for their
right and justice. Having said that, I appeal to the French government and all
protectors of secularism to repudiate this action and reject it fully and
fairly.

Allah Almighty knows best.





### Covering the bosom as part of _ḥijāb_ requirement

\QUESTION{174}{1}{7}{Submitted by Sayed on Jan 5, 2004}

I am a lady who strives hard to follow the Islamic teachings. My question is
about wearing _ḥijāb_. I have read that veils are to be drawn over our bosoms.
Is it _haram_ if the head is properly covered and the ends of the scarf are tied
behind and the bosoms are properly covered with the dress I wear?

\ANSWER

In the Name of Allah, Most Gracious, Most Merciful.
All praise and thanks are due to Allah, and peace and blessings be upon His
Messenger.

Certainly, this is the required _ḥijāb_ in the view of the majority of Islamic
schools of thought, as Allah said in the Qurʾān: “And tell the believing women
to…draw their veils over their chests and not reveal their charms...” (Al-Nūr,
24:31).

In addition to that the Prophet \hon{\PBUH}{\BODY} told his in-law that nothing
should be seen of the body of a matured woman except face and hands. Therefore,
what you said isn’t _haram_ as long as the head and bosoms are properly covered.

Allah Almighty knows best.





### Accessorising the _ḥijāb_

\QUESTION{175}{2}{7}{Submitted by Ahmed on Jul 19, 2004}

_As-Salāmu ʿalaykum_! I think the basic message of veil is not to attract men,
but what do you say to women who make their veil attractive by accessories? What
type of veil should one wear?

\ANSWER

_Waʿalaykum as-Salām wa raḥmatullāhi wa barakātuh_.

In the Name of Allah, Most Gracious, Most Merciful.
All praise and thanks are due to Allah, and peace and blessings be upon His
Messenger.

Surely, you are right; the veil was not meant to attract the attention of men
but, rather, to protect women as well as men. In other words, the veil is
considered as a protection for both genders. That means, when a woman wears a
veil, she is not protecting herself only, but she is also protecting the man
from committing any wrongdoing.

In this regard, a Muslim woman should do her best to make her veil more decent
and respected. She should not wear any veil that will be attractive in its
nature. At the same time, Islam calls upon Muslim men to lower their gaze and
guard their modesty. Allah says “Tell the believing men to lower their gaze and
be modest. That is purer for them. Lo! Allah is Aware of what they do,” (Al-Nūr,
24:30). The verse means that they should not be looking at women and observing
the way they dress.

As for what type of veil one should wear, I say that the veil must be decent,
not transparent or attractive in its nature. Having said that, I would point out
that the determination of approved color of the veil is left to the custom and
tradition in Muslim communities; that is to say, whatever is approved by custom
in terms of color, one should follow.

Allah Almighty knows best.

Kindly support your answer with evidence.

\ANSWER

In the Name of Allah, Most Gracious, Most Merciful.
All praise and thanks are due to Allah, and peace and blessings be upon His
Messenger.

Certainly, your wife must observe the _ḥijāb_ in the presence of her cousin, as

obligation. Furthermore, such an illness does not necessarily diminish his
sexual feelings or attraction towards women. In other words, his condition does
not necessarily affect his masculinity or sexual desires. Therefore, the Islamic
rulings governing interactions between non-_maḥram_ men and women remain
applicable.

Allah Almighty knows best.

## Beautification in Islam

### Women wearing make-up/cosmetics

\QUESTION{178}{5}{7}{Submitted by Ayisha on Jan 5, 2004}

To what extent should a Muslim woman wear makeup/cosmetics?

\ANSWER

In the Name of Allah, Most Gracious, Most Merciful.
All praise and thanks are due to Allah, and peace and blessings be upon His
Messenger.

A Muslim woman is allowed to wear makeup that does not attract strangers. This
is in light of the legal maxim that they means leading to evil should be
blocked. Wearing makeup is not forbidden unless it leads to evil. That will
normally occur when makeup is attractive and provocative in its nature. That
being said, there is no harm for a woman to wear decent makeup.

Allah Almighty knows best.





### Women wearing wigs or artificial hair

\QUESTION{179}{6}{7}{Submitted by Folakemi from Nigeria on Jul 28, 2003}

We learn from the _hadith_ that a woman is not supposed to wear artificial hair.
What if she does so? Can she say her prayers while wearing it? Also, what
alternative does a woman have if she cannot find water to perform ghusl?

\ANSWER

In the Name of Allah, Most Gracious, Most Merciful.
All praise and thanks are due to Allah, and peace and blessings be upon His
Messenger.

Surely, it is forbidden for a woman to wear artificial hair. In Islam it is
regarded as a form of cheating, and cheating is forbidden. The Prophet
\hon{\PBUH}{\BODY} said, “Whoever cheats us does not belong to us.” He also said
that Allah curses those women who wear artificial hair and those who prepare it
for them. Therefore, it is _haram_ to do so. Thus, it is clear that it is
forbidden for a woman to wear artificial hair. If she does so, she is considered
to be cursed by Allah as the Prophet \hon{\PBUH}{\BODY} stated. As for her
prayer, she has to remove the artificial hair for the sake of perfection and
acceptance of Allah.

As for a woman who cannot find water to perform _ghusl_, she is entitled to do
_tayammum_. This _tayammum_ is the same as the _tayammum_ for _wuḍūʾ_; the only
difference is at the level of the intention. She should intend to remove the
major impurity. This is mentioned in the Qurʾān: “O believers! When you rise up
for prayer, wash your faces and your hands up to the elbows, wipe your heads,
and wash your feet to the ankles. And if you are in a state of ˹full˺ impurity,
then take a full bath. But if you are ill, on a journey, or have relieved
yourselves, or have been intimate with your wives and cannot find water, then
purify yourselves with clean earth by wiping your faces and hands,” (Al-Māʾidah,
5:6).

Allah Almighty knows best.




### Women beautifying their faces/ Islamic stance on plastic surgery

\QUESTION{180}{7}{7}{Submitted by Sajjad from Alaska on Jul 5, 2004}

_As-Salāmu ʿalaykum_, I would to like to know if it is allowed to beautify one’s
face in a beauty parlor?

\ANSWER

_Wa ʿalaykum as-Salām wa raḥmatullāhi wa barakātuh_.

In the Name of Allah, Most Gracious, Most Merciful.
All praise and thanks are due to Allah, and peace and blessings be upon His
Messenger.

The beautification of the face or any part of the body should be based on the
following conditions:

1. It should be for the removal of any extra-ordinary part in the face, such as,
   extra flesh.

2. It should be based on the recommendations of an honest reliable doctor who
   would confirm that the existence of that part in the face would cause harm or
   hardship to the person.

In this regard, the beautification would be considered as medical treatment, and
medical treatment is permissible and sometimes obligatory from the Islamic
perspective. However, the beautification should not be done with the intention
of having more beauty. One should content themselves with the form that Allah
Almighty created them with, and they should avoid falling under the prohibitions
mentioned in the following Qurʾānic verse: “[Satan said:] ‘I will command them
to tamper with Allah’s creation.’ And whoever takes Satan as a guardian instead
of Allah has certainly suffered a tremendous loss,” (Al-Nisāʾ, 4:119).

Allah Almighty knows best.

## Miscellaneous Matters

### Women traveling without a _maḥram_

\QUESTION{181}{8}{7}{Submitted by Salma from Canada on Mar 29, 2004}

I am a university student and have been studying languages for the past two
years in a Muslim country. This year---as a result of our outstanding
performance---two of my classmates (girls) and I have been granted scholarships
to study a six-month-long course in a European country as part of the whole
four-year program. My father cannot accompany me due to his responsibilities
towards other members of the family and his job, which makes it impossible for
him to travel. My brother---with whom I live---can accompany me both going and
coming back, but he cannot stay with me for the whole six months since he also
studies. My father is willing to support me in every way and take all possible
measures and precautions to ensure that I am safe and secure---as long as it is
permissible according to the teachings of Islam for me to stay in this
non-Muslim country for this period of time to study. I would be staying on
campus grounds in the university dorm with the two other Muslim girls who have
also been granted the same scholarship.

\ANSWER

In the Name of Allah, Most Gracious, Most Merciful.
All praise and thanks are due to Allah, and peace and blessings be upon His
Messenger.

If I understand your situation correctly, I would not recommend you to think
about traveling alone and staying for few months without a _maḥram_. However, you
could be allowed to travel if you are sure that the place is going to be safe
and that the course would be beneficial to you. Having a few other girls in your
company is good and a positive sign. You should remember that such a journey has
to be necessary; otherwise, it would be better to avoid troubles and
uncertainties ahead of you. Islam doesn’t allow a woman, especially a young one,
to travel alone unless traveling is urgent and necessary such as traveling to
seek knowledge that is not available in her own place. This is due to the fact
that a woman would always need a protector for her decency and dignity. As you
may know, there is a lot of risk for a woman to live without a protector. All in
all, think well and decide wisely.

Allah Almighty knows best.

### Polygamy and women

\QUESTION{183}{10}{7}{Submitted by Albania on Apr 21, 2003}

Muslim scholars say that Allah says men and women are equal. Very conveniently,
they also say that equality and similarity are not the same. True, men cannot
give birth, and women cannot be expected to do some of things that men do. But
I’m sure that in front of God the feelings of a man and a woman are the same and
that their opinions are valued the same, as both are humans and both deserve
equal respect for their feelings. But it looks like Islam doesn’t consider that
because a man doesn’t even need his wife’s permission to marry again. This shows
that, whether she likes it or not, he can marry again! That is the value given
to a woman, and then that is altered to make excuses for equality. As for the
marriage clause in the marriage contract that a husband will not marry another
wife, most jurists do not say it has binding power on a man. Does the Sharīʿah
accept that clause, and can you comment on the above?

\ANSWER

In the Name of Allah, Most Gracious, Most Merciful.
All praise and thanks are due to Allah, and peace and blessings be upon His
Messenger.

As far as polygamy is concerned, Islam allows it in certain cases and with
certain conditions that are mainly based on justice and the ability of the man
to handle more than one wife. However, it is strongly recommended that a man
inform his first wife about it in order for her to know his whereabouts when he
is not at home. Yet, getting his first wife’s permission is the most ideal and
highly recommended one. However, it is exceedingly difficult for a lady to give
such permission. As for the clause in the marriage not to marry with another
one, there is a juristic dispute among scholars about the legitimacy of this
condition. The majority of them are of the opinion that this condition is
invalid and man is not obliged to respect it. I do believe that the man must
respect this condition if he accepted freely and willingly. This is based on the
prophetic statement that says: “You have to honor all your [contractual]
conditions, especially those conditions by which marital relations become
permissible with a woman.” Based on this, such a clause shall be binding if it
was accepted freely and willingly. Conversely, if it was accepted under
coercion, then the husband is not obliged to honor it.

Allah Almighty knows best.
