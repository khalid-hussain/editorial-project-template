# Spiritual Affairs

## Purification of the Soul

### How to overcome evil thoughts?

\QUESTION{1}{1}{1}{Submitted on July 19, 2004}

_As-Salāmu ʿalaykum_.
Is thinking of having an intimate relationship with someone a sin? For example,
if one dreams about such things, would one be held accountable for them as bad
deeds?

\ANSWER

In the Name of Allah, Most Gracious, Most Merciful.
All praise and thanks are due to Allah, and peace and blessings be upon His
Messenger.

Islam stands for good morals and behaviour. It calls upon its followers to avoid
any deeds and actions that lead to evil. However, while thinking of engaging in
an unlawful intimate relationship with someone is a bad thought, it is not
sinful unless it ultimately leads one to commit the sin. In other words, if you
refrain from acting upon such a bad thought, you will be rewarded, as stated by
the Prophet \hon{\PBUH}{\BODY}: “Whoever thinks about doing a bad deed but does
not do it will be rewarded.”

In this context, you should try your best to avoid such bad thoughts, which
might lead you to commit a sin. I recommend that you get married as soon as
possible if you can afford to do so. However, if you are not in a financial or
physical position to marry, you should fast from time to time, especially on
Mondays and Thursdays.

This will help you overcome bad thoughts and dreams. Moreover, I encourage you
to occupy your time with work and communication with learners and friends in
order to overcome such thoughts and dreams.

May Allah grant you good health and protect you against evil and devils.

Allah Almighty knows best.



### Self-purification (_Tazkīyah_)

\QUESTION{2}{2}{1}{Submitted by Saʿeed from the United States on Feb 25, 2003}

For the last couple of years, I’ve had a growing interest in purification of the
self (_tazkīyah_). I was wondering: what are the best ways to learn about this
subject? If you have specific suggestions for books in the English language, it
would be greatly appreciated. Arabic books are also valuable, as I can
understand enough to get some benefit.

\ANSWER

In the Name of Allah, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful.
All praise is due to Allah, and may peace and blessings be upon His Messenger.

The purification of the self (_tazkīyat al-nafs_) is among the noblest objectives
of Islam, as it seeks to cultivate sincerity, strengthen faith, refine
character, and free the heart from spiritual and moral shortcomings. Allah
Almighty says: “Successful indeed is the one who purifies their soul” (Qurʾān
91:9). The best approach to learning about this subject is to combine the study
of the Qurʾān and Sunnah with the reading of reliable scholarly works and, above
all, the practical application of their teachings in daily life. I would
particularly recommend the following works:

1. Imam al-Ghazālī, _Iḥyāʾ ʿUlūm al-Dīn_ (The Revival of the Religious Sciences):
   A comprehensive classical work on spiritual refinement, sincerity,
   self-discipline, and the purification of the heart. Selected sections are
   available in English.

2. Imam Ibn al-Qayyim, _Madārij al-Sālikīn_ (Stations of the Seekers): A profound
   examination of spiritual development, sincerity, repentance, reliance upon
   Allah, and the stages of drawing closer to Him. English translations of parts
   of this work are available.

3. Imam Ibn al-Qayyim, _Al-Dāʾ wa al-Dawāʾ_ (The Disease and the Cure): A valuable
   treatment of spiritual diseases, their causes, consequences, and remedies,
   available in English.

4. Imam al-Muḥāsibī, _Al-Riʿāyah li-Ḥuqūq Allāh_ (Observing the Rights of Allah):
   An important early work on self-examination, sincerity, vigilance, and moral
   accountability, particularly beneficial for readers of Arabic.

5. Imam al-Nawawī, _Riyāḍ al-Ṣāliḥīn_ (Gardens of the Righteous): An accessible
   collection of authentic Prophetic teachings on sincerity, repentance,
   patience, humility, remembrance of Allah, and virtuous conduct, widely
   available in English.

I would especially encourage you to begin with _Riyāḍ al-Ṣāliḥīn_ and selected
chapters of al-Ghazālī's _Iḥyāʾ ʿUlūm al-Dīn_, before proceeding to more
detailed works.

Above all, remember that the true purpose of _tazkīyah_ is not merely to acquire
knowledge about spiritual purification, but to translate that knowledge into
sincere worship, upright conduct, constant self-examination, and obedience to
Allah. May Allah Almighty purify our hearts, refine our character, and grant us
sincerity in our words and deeds.

Allah Almighty knows best.

### Characteristics of the imam

\QUESTION{11}{11}{1}{Submitted by Abdur Raheem from the United Kingdom on May 12, 2003}

The imam who leads the community in our Jumuʿah prayers has cited weak hadiths and false stories. Can we pray behind him? What shall we do?

\ANSWER

In the Name of Allah, Most Gracious, Most Merciful.
All praise and thanks are due to Allah, and peace and blessings be upon His Messenger.

Good imams are expected to have wide knowledge of the hadiths that they discuss.
However, there is no harm in mentioning a weak hadith that doesn’t contain an
obligation or prohibition that is not supported by other evidence. Citing a weak
hadith is warranted when it helps in strengthening or fostering the belief and
fear of Allah. However, the community imam should not state any false stories as
it is forbidden to do so. Having said this, I add that stating false stories or
weak hadiths should not change the status of the imam. Thus, you should continue
praying behind him.

Furthermore, you should try to inform him about the status of those weak hadiths
or false stories. You need to employ wisdom in reminding or correcting this type
of mistake from your imam.

Of course, you should also know that scholars have differing opinions about the
authenticity of many hadiths, meaning that a hadith could be weak according to a
certain imam, but the same hadith could be authentic according to another imam.

Therefore, you should be certain about the weakness of a hadith before raising
an objection. For instance, Sheikh Al-Albānī (may Allah bless his soul) has
graded many hadiths as weak which were considered authentic according to Imam
Al-Tirmidhī, Imam  Al-Nasāʾī, Imam  Abū Dāwūd and Imam Ibn Mājah. Some other
scholars have graded certain hadiths as weak that Al-Albānī graded authentic.
All in all, there should not be a fight or dispute about this matter.

Allah Almighty knows best.
