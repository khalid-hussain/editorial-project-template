# Economic Affairs

## Can Muslims work in finance institutions?

\QUESTION{113}{1}{4}{Submitted by Omar on Feb 25, 2003}

Is the job of an actuary who mainly calculates insurance premiums _haram_ or
_halal_?

\ANSWER

In the Name of Allah, Most Gracious, Most Merciful.
All praise and thanks are due to Allah, and peace and blessings be upon His Messenger.

The basic ruling for actuarial work is that it is _halal_ because it is equal to
any technical work in a company. It is a very normal job. However, I shall
mention here that a commercial insurance company is considered a prohibited
company. Based on that, one may conclude that working as an actuary in such a
company is a form of contributing to the promotion of _haram_. I am of the
opinion that this issue is equal to working in conventional banks which function
on the basis of _ribā_.

Allah Almighty knows best.

## Can Muslims have a business that sells wine?

\QUESTION{115}{3}{4}{Submitted by Nazier from South Africa on Aug 25, 2003}

_As-Salāmu ʿalaykum_. A Muslim owns a four-star hotel, and he is forced to sell
wine in order to have a business, but he keeps the wine money separately in
order not to use it for lawful things, i.e., to pay salaries, rent, food, etc.
Is it permissible to do so, particularly where the Muslim community is a
minority?

\ANSWER

_Wa ʿalaykum as-Salām wa raḥmatullāhi wa barakātuh_.

In the Name of Allah, Most Gracious, Most Merciful.
All praise and thanks are due to Allah, and peace and blessings be upon His Messenger.

May Allah help you and guide all of us. Islam prohibits a Muslim from selling
wine or drinking it. If he or she sells it, they are committing a sin as clearly
stated in the Qurʾān and many _hadiths_ of the Prophet \hon{\PBUH}{\BODY}. One
of these _hadiths_ states: “Allah curses the drinker of the wine, its seller, its
distributor, etc.”

So, as a Muslim, you have to do your best not to sell wine. Here, I do not see
any obligation or force to sell wine. For instance, it is up to you to determine
foods and drinks you want to provide in your four-star hotel. There is no law
that obliges you to sell wine. Therefore, you have to try to uphold your
religion and generate money from _halal_ (lawful) sources only.

As for money you have collected from selling wine, you should know that it is
not _halal_ and you have to do your best to donate it to the nearest needy
Muslim association or organisation. Certainly, you have to repent to your Lord
for selling wine. In addition to that, you have to commit yourself to avoid
doing that from now onwards. May Allah grant you strength and faith (_īmān_) to
do so.

Allah Almighty knows best.



\QUESTION{116}{4}{4}{Submitted by Rosemary from the United States on May 21, 2006}

Scholars of Islam, _as-Salāmu ʿalaykum_. Recently, I read in the news that some
Muslim scholars say the Ḥanafī School of fiqh may allow Muslims to sell alcohol
in a non-Muslim country. If this is true, what are the conditions and what is
the proof of that ruling?

\ANSWER

As for your question, I would like to inform you that, to the best of my
knowledge, there is no such a statement in the Ḥanafī School; thus, there is a
consensus among scholars about the prohibition of selling or buying or drinking
alcohol.

As you may know well, alcohol is prohibited and is considered as the mother of
all sins and impurities, as the Prophet \hon{\PBUH}{\BODY} stated.

All in all, Muslims are not allowed to sell or buy or drink or even facilitate
the process of selling or buying or transporting alcohol. Allah has prohibited
alcohol in the verse “O believers! Intoxicants, gambling, idols, and drawing
lots for decisions are all evil of Satan’s handiwork. So, shun them so you may
be successful,” (Al-Māʾidah, 5:90).

The prohibition in the above verse covers selling alcohol as well as buying it.

Having said that, I point out to you that there should be no consideration to
any view or opinion that allows selling or buying alcohol for Muslims or
non-Muslims.

Allah Almighty knows best.



## Accepting business invitations where alcohol is served

\QUESTION{117}{5}{4}{Submitted by Farhat on Sep 14, 2004}

My in-laws are very religious. They have lots of agencies for airlines. They
also have lots of other businesses, like Islamic banking, etc. Recently they
were awarded by Virgin Atlantic Airline for being agents. The Virgin Atlantic
had a very big cocktail party and lots of people were invited, so our group also
had invited their customers. They did not spend any money towards the party, but
is it correct for them to be involved where alcohol was served and women were
massaging guests to promote their flight services. Can you please advise?

\ANSWER

In the Name of Allah, Most Gracious, Most Merciful.
All praise and thanks are due to Allah, and peace and blessings be upon His Messenger.

Thank you for your question. Islam stands for cooperation and communication with
others. It also calls upon all Muslims to mix with others in order to introduce
Islam to their partners. In this regard, there is no harm for you in attending a
party organised by Virgin Atlantic Airline. Having said that, you should not
take part in any prohibited activities such as drinking or serving alcohol to
those who want to drink it.

Furthermore, you should observe the teachings of Islam in terms of your
relations with women who are not close relatives. Islam prohibits any illegal
physical contact with a woman who is not a close relative. Try to do your best
for Islam while interacting or attending such occasions. May Allah give you
strength and protection always.

Allah Almighty knows best.



## Are there any circumstances where _ribā_ is permissible?

\QUESTION{118}{6}{4}{Submitted by Frank from the United States on Apr 29, 2003}

Are there any circumstances when _ribā_ is _halal_?

\ANSWER

In the Name of Allah, Most Gracious, Most Merciful.
All praise and thanks are due to Allah, and peace and blessings be upon His Messenger.

As far as Islam is concerned, _ribā_ is not _halal_ at all. It is considered a
heinous sin that Allah has clearly and explicitly prohibited. It is not allowed
in any circumstances.

Muslims have to avoid it and all means leading to it in their financial and
economic transactions. Allah Almighty says: “Those who consume interest will
stand on Judgment Day like those driven to madness by Satan’s touch. That is
because they say, ‘Trade is no different than interest.’ But Allah has permitted
trading and forbidden interest. Whoever refrains---after having received warning
from their Lord---may keep their previous gains, and their case is left to
Allah. As for those who persist, they will be the residents of the Fire. There
they will remain forever. Allah has made interest fruitless and charity
fruitful. And Allah does not like any ungrateful evildoer. Indeed, those who
believe, do good, establish prayer, and pay alms-tax will receive their reward
from their Lord, and there will be no fear for them, nor will they grieve,”
(Al-Baqarah, 2:275--278).

Allah Almighty knows best.



## Muslims working for non-Muslims

\QUESTION{119}{7}{4}{Submitted by Abdur Raheem on Feb 25, 2003}

_As-Salāmu ʿalaykum_, Can a Muslim work for a non-Muslim police force?

\ANSWER

_Waʿalaykum as-Salām wa raḥmatullāhi wa barakātuh_.

In the Name of Allah, Most Gracious, Most Merciful.
All praise and thanks are due to Allah, and peace and blessings be upon His Messenger.

There is no harm if a Muslim works for a non-Muslim police force for the sake of
protecting the country and the nation. He should try his best to promote Islam
among non-Muslim police officers as much as he can. Furthermore, he will have to
do his best to uphold justice, security and peace, and to display a true image
of Islam all the time.

Taking into account the current status of Muslims in many non-Muslim countries,
Muslims are strongly recommended to join the police and military forces in their
respective countries in order to defend their fellow Muslims in the future and
to assist other Muslims in the world when they will take leading roles in the
future.

Allah Almighty knows best.

## Working in a medical profession that requires seeing the _ʿawrah_ of a person

\QUESTION{121}{9}{4}{Submitted by Karima from Sweden on Mar 31, 2003}

My job is giving medical help to men and women in the hospital or in their
houses. Sometimes they are without clothes. It’s the only job that I can have,
and I need to work. Is this job _halal_ or _haram_? Please I need the answer!

\ANSWER

In the Name of Allah, Most Gracious, Most Merciful.
All praise and thanks are due to Allah, and peace and blessings be upon His Messenger.

For the sake of offering medical or health treatment to those people who are in
need of that, it is _halal_ in Islam to see them without clothes if taking off
their clothes is necessary for treatment.

This is based on the legal maxim that says: “Necessities render the prohibited
permissible”. May Allah help you and give you a long life and good health. I
wish you all the best in your job.

Having stated the above, I should add that it would be better to avoid seclusion
(_khalwah_) as much as you can. I mean that you should do your treatment in the
presence of the wife if the patient is a man. I do believe that being a lady you
need to take this part into consideration when you treat a man. May Allah
protect you.

Allah Almighty knows best.
