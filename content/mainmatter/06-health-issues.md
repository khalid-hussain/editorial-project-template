# Health Issues

## Halal food and products

### Eating out with non-Muslim friends and colleagues

\QUESTION{165}{1}{6}{Submitted by Jamal from Canada on Apr 21, 2003}

_As-Salāmu ʿalaykum_, I often go out for lunch and dinner with professional and
personal friends, and I often find myself in an awkward position of stressing
over what to order. I only eat _halal_, but at most restaurants the only things
I can eat are either vegetarian or fish dishes which are not to my taste at all,
or the non-_halal_ chicken and beef items. My question is: should I just order
the vegetarian dishes, which I eat with great hesitation---and often end up
returning the dish, feeling guilty about wasting food and money---or can I order
the chicken and beef dishes on occasions strictly limited to such as this one,
to avoid drawing undue attention and feeling uncomfortable throughout the whole
situation? _Jazākum Allah khayran_.

\ANSWER

_Wa ʿalaykum as-Salām wa raḥmatullāhi wa barakātuh_.

In the Name of Allah, Most Gracious, Most Merciful.
All praise and thanks are due to Allah, and peace and blessings be upon His
Messenger.

Dear brother, Thank you for your question and may Allah bless you. You should
just order the vegetarian dishes and avoid non-_halal_ chicken and beef.
Furthermore, try to find a restaurant which provides _halal_ food. By doing so,
you will avoid all the problems stated in your question. May Allah guide you and
grant you strong _īmān_.

Allah Almighty knows best.

### Ingredients in food

\QUESTION{166}{2}{6}{Submitted by Ikmal from US on Mar 31, 2003}

I live in America. What should I do when I find out some of the food that I have
eaten was not halal? And what should I do if my soap and toothpaste contains
pork? Should I wash my mouth and body with mud as the religious practice
requires? Your reply is highly appreciated. Regards!

\ANSWER

In the Name of Allah, Most Gracious, Most Merciful.
All praise and thanks are due to Allah, and peace and blessings be upon His
Messenger.

As for food that you have eaten without knowing that it was not _halal_, you
should not be worried about it. Almighty Allah will forgive you this mistake and
you just need to repent to Him and try your best to find _halal_ food in the
future. As for soap and toothpaste that contain pork, you should try to find
soap and toothpaste that are free from this item. They are available, by the
grace of Allah.

Allah Almighty knows best.



\QUESTION{167}{3}{6}{Submitted by K on Apr 21, 2003}

I have a question about the yeast in the bread that is sold in the supermarkets
here in Europe. Some people say that some types of yeast are grown on liquor.
Can we buy the bread in the supermarkets?

\ANSWER

In the Name of Allah, Most Gracious, Most Merciful.
All praise and thanks are due to Allah, and peace and blessings be upon His
Messenger.

As far as the bread is concerned, it is alright to buy it from the supermarkets
in Europe. There is nothing wrong in that _inshāʾ Allah_. As for the issue of
yeast, I don’t see any problem with the yeast that is used in brewing and baking
as long as it doesn’t dominate the bread itself. Of course, if the bread turns
into an alcoholic item, then it becomes prohibited.

Allah Almighty knows best.



\QUESTION{168}{4}{6}{Submitted by Mohammad from India on Apr 29, 2003}

Respected Sheikh, I would like to ask if it is permissible to eat the chickens
with the _halal_ label on the cover in the Gulf markets which are imported from
different countries as well as domestic production. To the best of my knowledge,
the chickens are slaughtered using machines and not by people uttering
_bismillah_ (In the Name of Allah). Are the chickens slaughtered without any
person pronouncing _bismillah_ _halal_ or not? Thanks.

\ANSWER

In the Name of Allah, Most Gracious, Most Merciful.
All praise and thanks are due to Allah, and peace and blessings be upon His
Messenger.

It is _halal_ to eat any chicken with the _halal_ label on the cover, unless one
finds out that the statement is not true and is, in fact, false. As for chickens
slaughtered without uttering _bismillah_, they are _halal_ if the person who
slaughtered them is a Muslim.

Allah Almighty knows best.


\QUESTION{169}{5}{6}{Submitted on Jul 28, 2003}

I want to know if it is permissible to use a cream that has urea or uric acid in
it? I use foot cream which really helps in softening the heels. Guided by my
experience, I can say that the only creams that work in softening are the ones
that have this ingredient. I don’t know if it’s processed or not. If it’s
processed, will it be permissible?

\ANSWER

In the Name of Allah, Most Gracious, Most Merciful.
All praise and thanks are due to Allah, and peace and blessings be upon His
Messenger.

As far as softening the heels is concerned, it is permissible to use the said
cream. Uric acid is regarded as impurity, but for the sake of medical treatment,
it should be allowed and permitted whether it is processed or not. This is based
on the legal maxim that says: “Necessities render the prohibited permissible.”

Allah Almighty knows best.


\QUESTION{170}{6}{6}{Submitted by Ali from the United States on Aug 25, 2003}

As a Muslim living in the USA, can we eat anything cooked in oil made from lard,
such as donuts, or not? Thanks.

\ANSWER

In the Name of Allah, Most Gracious, Most Merciful.
All praise and thanks are due to Allah, and peace and blessings be upon His
Messenger. A

A Muslim should avoid items cooked in oil made from lard. Islam forbids pork and
lard, as clearly stated in verses 2:172, 5:3, 6:145, 16:115. For example: “He
has only forbidden you to eat carrion, blood, swine, and what is slaughtered in
the name of any other than Allah,” (Al-Baqarah, 2:172).

Thus, you have to avoid eating food that is cooked in oil made from lard as much
as you can.

Allah Almighty knows best.



### Food prepared by non-Muslims

\QUESTION{171}{7}{6}{Submitted by Mona on Apr 29, 2003}

I live in Canada, and there are some _halal_ food stores here, so I can always
cook _halal_ food at home, but what should I do when I’m invited to social events
or lunches with my colleagues at work? Also, I have many Christian friends who
buy their food in the local supermarket where everybody else buys their food. I
share food with them, and they share food with me. If we go out for a picnic,
everyone brings food. I find it hard to be social and interact with my
colleagues who are from different ethnic groups and with my Christians friends
who eat the same meat and chicken that everybody else buys and eats here. I
mean, it’s not courteous to refuse eating their meat or chicken meals, because
they’ll do the same with me. I’m only talking about chicken and beef. Is it
_halal_ for me, when I’m interacting with the rest of the population, to just say
“Bismillāh” before I eat the meat? _Jazākum Allah khayran_.

\ANSWER

In the Name of Allah, Most Gracious, Most Merciful.
All praise and thanks are due to Allah, and peace and blessings be upon His
Messenger.

A Muslim should do their best to locate where lawful food is served. Therefore,
you should try your best to find _halal_ food. And in the same vein, you should
exert yourself to convey the message of Islam to your friends along with its
practical instructions about everything in general and foods in particular.
However, if you fail to find _halal_ food, then you may consider the other
option, which is to pronounce the _tasmiyah_ (i.e., saying “In the Name of
Allah, Most Gracious, Most Merciful) when you eat meat or chicken without
probing if they were slaughtered according to the Islamic principles.

Allah Almighty knows best.



\QUESTION{172}{8}{6}{Submitted by Yaqub from Nigeria on Apr 29, 2003}

Can we eat the food presented to us by the People of the Book who are our
neighbors during the days of their festivals?

\ANSWER

In the Name of Allah, Most Gracious, Most Merciful.
All praise and thanks are due to Allah, and peace and blessings be upon His
Messenger.

It is allowed to eat the food of the People of the Book as long as that food is
not prohibited for us Muslims. If the food presented to us by the People of the
Book is considered prohibited for us, then one shouldn’t eat it. This is based
on Qurʾānic verse that reads: “Today all good, pure foods have been made lawful
for you. Similarly, the food of the People of the Book is permissible for you
and yours is permissible for them. And permissible for you in marriage are
chaste believing women as well as chaste women of those given the Scripture
before you---as long as you pay them their dowries in wedlock, neither
fornicating nor taking them as mistresses. And whoever rejects the faith, all
their good deeds will be void ˹in this life˺ and in the Hereafter they will be
among the losers,” (Al-Māʾidah, 5:5).

Having said this, I would like to say that there is nothing wrong in eating the
food presented to us by the People of the Book if such food is originally lawful
for us to consume and eat.

Allah Almighty knows best.
