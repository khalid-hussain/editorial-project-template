# Educational Affairs

## The Qurʾān

### Authenticity of the Qurʾān

\QUESTION{100}{1}{3}{Submitted by S.A. on Jul 19, 2004}

_As-Salāmu ʿalaykum_!

My question is that I have heard the Qurʾān was written down or compiled ten
years after the death of Prophet Muhammad \hon{\PBUH}{\BODY}. Is it true?

This question was addressed to me by one of my Christian classmates. He said: if
the Qurʾān was not written at the time of Prophet Muhammad, then how do you
believe that it is the true word of God? If it was sent to Prophet Muhammad but
he was not there when it was compiled, how can Muslims believe in it?

I told him that the companions of Prophet Muhammad \hon{\PBUH}{\BODY} memorised
the Qurʾān during his lifetime and then compiled it. But he needed more details
and proof. My question here is: Can you provide me with good proof and
explanation so I can explain the issue to him in a better way? I will be really
thankful. _Jazākum Allah khayran_.

\ANSWER

_Waʿalaykum as-Salām wa raḥmatullāhi wa barakātuh_.

In the Name of Allah, Most Gracious, Most Merciful.
All praise and thanks are due to Allah, and peace and blessings be upon His Messenger.

Thank you and may Allah grant you more knowledge. You were very right regarding
what you told your classmate. However, you should differentiate between writing
and compiling. The Holy Qurʾān was compiled after the death of the Prophet
\hon{\PBUH}{\BODY}, i.e., it was fully written and protected at the time of the
Prophet, and it was also fully memorised by many people at that time. In
addition, Allah has promised that He will protect this book against all forms
and types of destruction and cheating: “It is certainly We Who have revealed the
Reminder, and it is certainly We Who will preserve it,” (Al-Ḥijr, 15:9). This
divine promise has always been there.

Thus, the Holy Qurʾān is the only scripture that has not been subject to
falsification or amendments. Furthermore, the first compilation of Qurʾān
occurred less than three years after the death of the Prophet
\hon{\PBUH}{\BODY}, not after ten years as your good friend mentioned.

All in all, the Holy Qurʾān has been proven throughout history as an accurate
and consistent book of God. All attempts to amend or falsify its contents have
failed. Yet, the Qurʾān does not contain any teachings and commands that go
against the nature or the interest of mankind. Finally, request your friend to
go through the Glorious Qurʾān. Surely, he will find it a wonderful and
interesting book.

Allah Almighty knows best.




### Are there any abrogated verses in the Qurʾān?

\QUESTION{101}{2}{3}{Submitted by Sayyeda on Mar 29, 2004}

I am sad to say that this is the first time that I have started to read the Holy
Qurʾān, and I have to read it in English. I am a little confused about
something. I am only on the seventh _sūrah_, and already I have read two verses
about which the footnote comments state that they have been “abrogated”,
“cancelled”, or “amended”. Why, and how did the commentators/ interpreters come
to the conclusion that these verses were cancelled by other verses? I find it
hard to believe that anything in the Qurʾān needs “amending” or “cancelling”!
Could you please shed some light on this topic? Thank you.

\ANSWER

In the Name of Allah, Most Gracious, Most Merciful.
All praise and thanks are due to Allah, and peace and blessings be upon His Messenger.

Thanks a lot for this interesting question. I would like to congratulate you for
starting to read the Holy Qurʾān, May Allah, the Almighty, give more knowledge
and strong courage to continue doing so.

As for your question, the word abrogation is a translation of the word _naskh_,
which means revocation and replacement of a legal ruling with another legal
ruling. It doesn’t mean cancellation or amendment or the verses in the Holy
Qurʾān in its real sense.

However, this issue has been a subject of disagreement among scholars. I am of
the opinion that there is no cancellation or abrogation but there was always
revocation or progression in setting up legal rulings. In other words, the
Qurʾān was revealed to the Prophet \hon{\PBUH}{\BODY} gradually in order to
enable the people to have better understanding of its meanings and teachings.
This is stated in the Holy Qurʾān; Allah says: “It is a Qurʾān We have revealed
in stages so that you may recite it to people at a deliberate pace. And We have
sent it down in successive revelations,” (Al-Isrāʾ, 17:106).

However, you should not bother with this view or comment that claims that few
verses have been abrogated. But continue reading and studying the Holy Qurʾān.
Certainly, there is no doubt that what is in the _muṣḥaf_ (printed copy of the
Qurʾān) today is considered Qurʾān.

Allah Almighty knows best.

## The Hadith

### Is there a conflict between some _hadiths_ and science?

\QUESTION{105}{6}{3}{Submitted by S. M from Canada on Mar 25, 2003}

_As-Salām ʿalaykum_. I know that the Qurʾān came from Allah, and there is no
dispute about that. My question is about _hadith_. I was talking to a recent
revert brother, who was telling me about the book, _The Bible, the Qurʾān and
the Science_, by Dr. Maurice Bucaille. The author stated that the Qurʾān has
irrefutable scientific evidence and that all the scientific facts in the Qurʾān
agree with science but that many _hadiths_ of the Messenger of Allah
\hon{\PBUH}{\BODY} contradict science. Please, could you clarify how _hadiths_
contradict science?

\ANSWER

In the Name of Allah, Most Gracious, Most Merciful.
All praise and thanks are due to Allah, and peace and blessings be upon His Messenger.

I thank the questioner and pray to Allah to guide all of us and strengthen our
_īmān_. It is known that there are two types of revelation: the first one is
called _al-wahy al-ẓāhir_, meaning the clear and direct revelation; and the
second is called _al-wahy al-bāṭin_, meaning the hidden and indirect revelation.
The first one refers to the Glorious Qurʾān, while the second one refers to the
Sunnah of the Prophet \hon{\PBUH}{\BODY}. As Muslims, we are obliged to believe
in both, based on many verses in the Qurʾān such as: “Glory be to You! We have
no knowledge except what You have taught us. You are truly the All-Knowing,
All-Wise,” (Al-Baqarah, 2:32), “Obey Allah and obey the Messenger and beware!
But if you turn away, then know that Our Messenger’s duty is only to deliver the
message clearly,” (Al-Māʾidah, 5:92), “Indeed, the example of Jesus in the sight
of Allah is like that of Adam. He created him from dust, then said to him, 'Be!'
And he came to be,” (Āl ʿImrān, 3:59), and others. Accordingly, no belief is
considered acceptable before Allah if it is based on separation between these
two types of revelation.

However, there are differences between them in terms of their authenticity and
authority. The whole Qurʾān is known to be definitive and free from
falsification and forgery, while the Sunnah is divided into definitive and
speculative. The former is authentic and free from any falsification, while the
latter has been subject to falsification and forgery. But, unless a _hadith_ is
proven to be unauthentic, we have to believe in it and accept it.

As for the claim that many _hadiths_ contradict scientific realities, I say that
there is no such authentic _hadith_ at all, meaning, if a _hadith_ is found to
be in contradiction with scientific realities, this _hadith_ must be a false
one; or the so-called scientific reality is not, in fact, a reality. Therefore,
one will have to conclude that contradiction will never occur between an
authentic _hadith_ and a scientific reality, and if there is any contradiction,
it must be because of a misunderstanding of either the revelation or the
scientific reality, or both of them at the same time.

Based on this, I would like to confirm to our beloved brother that there is no
contradiction between an authentic _hadith_ and Qurʾānic verses or scientific
realities. Further, I would also like to clarify to him that Prophet Muhammad
\hon{\PBUH}{\BODY} never said any _hadith_ to suit his own needs. His actions
are considered to be an application of the meaning of the Qurʾān. Yet, it is
true that there are some Sunnahs of the Prophet \hon{\PBUH}{\BODY} that are
based on a particular culture and tradition, and such type of Sunnah is not
considered as legislation for Muslims at all. This type comprises his preference
in food, ways of talking, appearance and walking, the color and type of his
dress, etc. This type of Sunnah is known as non-legislative Sunnah, and it is
not a part of the religion, meaning nobody is obliged to follow it.

Finally, I recommend that our brother read a wonderful book written by Sheikh
Yūsuf al-Qaraḍāwī titled _How We Deal with the Sunnah_. Surely, he will get a
lot of knowledge and clarification about many things related to the Sunnah of
the Prophet \hon{\PBUH}{\BODY}.

May Allah guide all of us on the right path and strengthen our love of the
Prophet \hon{\PBUH}{\BODY}.

## Etiquette of the Seeker of Knowledge

\QUESTION{109}{10}{3}{Submitted by Sana on Jan 5, 2004}

I want to ask you about the relationship between the student and the teacher
from an Islamic perspective. I also want to ask you, how shall a student react
towards his teacher if he is being mistreated?

\ANSWER

In the Name of Allah, Most Gracious, Most Merciful.
All praise and thanks are due to Allah, and peace and blessings be upon His Messenger.

The relationship between teacher and student should be based on mutual love and
respect. A teacher should treat his students like his own sons, and the student
should consider his teacher like his own father.

In this regard, they should exchange respect and love within Islamic principles.
If a teacher has been mistreated, the student should do their best to assist the
teacher in getting back his right. However, one should make sure that their
teacher has indeed been mistreated based on evidence and proof, not on passion
and sympathy. Islam calls upon all Muslims to help their fellow Muslims when an
injustice is done against one of them. In this regard, students should do their
best to remove injustice done against their teacher.

Having said this, I can say that if the teacher has mistreated his student, the
student has the right to complain officially. However, the student should always
consider the teacher like his or her father. He should not try to put him in
trouble. He should find ways and means to get his rights without hurting his
teacher. Mutual understanding and tolerance are highly recommended in this case.

Surely, Islam calls upon teachers to be fair and honest. The teacher has to be a
model to be followed; he should not do injustice to his students. If he does so,
he will certainly face dire consequences on the Day of Judgment. Allah Almighty
knows best.
