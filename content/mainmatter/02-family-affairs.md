### Dower (_Mahr_)---to whom should it be given?

\QUESTION{71}{2}{2}{Submitted by Sana on Jul 5, 2004}

Sir, is it wrong to get a dowry from the girl’s side? If this is done when she
is getting married, will the marriage become invalid?

\ANSWER

In the Name of Allah, Most Gracious, Most Merciful.
All praise and thanks are due to Allah, and peace and blessings be upon His Messenger.
I pray to Allah that He facilitates marriage for those who seek it.

As far as the dowry is concerned, it should be coming from the man’s side. It is
an obligation upon him that he has to pay to the woman, and this dowry is a full
right of the woman, which totally belongs to her and not anybody else.

Dowry is considered one of the main conditions for the validity of the marriage
contract. It must be paid either at the beginning of the marriage or during the
marriage, and nobody should forgive it without the consent of the woman.

Therefore, the man has to pay the dowry to the woman, and it is up to them to
agree on the time of the payment. However, there is no harm from the girl to
assist the man willing to get married to her with a sum of money if he is
finding it difficult to get the money to pay the dowry.

I would point out that the dowry could be in kind as well as in cash. The dowry
must be paid, as Allah Almighty says, “Give women their bridal gift upon
marriage. But if they waive some of it willingly, you may enjoy it with a clear
conscience,” (Al-Nisāʾ, 4:4).

The payment of the dowry is not a prerequisite for the marriage; i.e., the
marriage can take place without the payment of the _mahr_ on the spot; it can be
paid later on, but there must be an agreement on the payment of the dowry. Allah
Almighty knows best.

### The importance of the guardian (_walī_) in a marriage

\QUESTION{72}{3}{2}{Submitted by Sandra from Indonesia on Sep 14, 2004}

I got married without a _walī_ at the age of 25 because the parents on both
sides did not agree to the marriage. After a year, my husband divorced me,
citing his parents’ refusal to approve our marriage as the reason.

During the marriage, he never gave me any financial support because he said I
have enough money from my own salary. Are all of these correct according to
Islam?

\ANSWER

In the Name of Allah, Most Gracious, Most Merciful.
All praise and thanks are due to Allah, and peace and blessings be upon His Messenger.

The majority of Islamic schools of jurisprudence consider a _walī_ as a
pre-condition for a legal marriage, while Ḥanafīs consider a _walī_ as a
pre-condition for an immature girl.

However, since you have already got married, you should seek your parents’
forgiveness and their blessings for your marriage. This is their right upon both
of you.

As for your husband who divorced you on the grounds that his parents refused
your marriage, this is not a valid or acceptable reason for divorce. The
agreement of his parents is not a requirement, nor is it a pre-condition at all
in any of the juristic schools. As for the _walī_, he has to fear Allah and
avoid doing wrong to the woman for whom he acts as a _walī_.

As for the husband’s claim that he need not give you any financial support, this
is not correct. In Islam, he is obliged to support you financially and provide
you with the basic needs such as food, housing, and clothes. He should not take
any advantage of your salary. It is yours, and he has no right to it.

All in all, I call upon your husband to fear Allah and avoid oppression and
injustice. As for your part, do your best to protect your marriage by pleasing
his parents if you can. If it turns out that the marriage cannot be saved,
realise that this is what Allah wrote for you and keep praying to Allah to give
you a better and more responsible husband. May Allah grant that.

Allah Almighty knows best.

### Disclosing a potential sexually transmitted disease (STD) in a potential spouse

\QUESTION{75}{6}{2}{Submitted by Alayan from Canada on Jul 1, 2003}

_zinā_ some time ago. I have repented, and I am now a strong, devout Muslim. I
carried out tests for STDs, and they were all negative, _al-ḥamdulillāh_. The
results showed no STDs. However, a number of doctors said there is still some
chance that someone can have genital warts without showing symptoms (even if a
condom had been used). _Al-ḥamdulillāh_, I do not show any symptoms, but warts
are one of those things that could grow on the skin later on.

Also, there are no tests for it, other than seeing them on the skin after they
have already developed. It is estimated that about 75% of those who have ever
had intercourse before have genital warts but do not show symptoms, and many
will never show symptoms. The doctors also said that even if warts do show on
the genitals (on the outside for males and inside for females), they are
relatively harmless. It was once believed that warts increase the risk of
cervical cancer for women, but it is now known that the warts that show on the
skin are not the types associated with cervical cancer; rather, it’s the types
that do not ever show on the skin. And even if a man passed that type on to his
wife, and this increased her risk of cervical cancer, today’s health care is
able to detect this before it actually becomes harmful to the woman, and
effective treatment can be given.

Also, if the warts do show on a man’s genitals, then a doctor can easily freeze
them off, just like any other wart a person gets on their body. The doctors also
said that a lot of those who have genital warts would develop them on the
genital skin within about 3--4 months (if they carry the warts in the first
place). _Al-ḥamdulillāh_, 4 months have almost passed now and I haven’t shown
any warts. I was told that, given my circumstances, my chances of carrying the
warts are low.

My questions are:

1.	Am I allowed to get married in the future given the possibility that I could
    develop warts on the skin of my genitals?

2.	Do I have to tell my future wife or her family of the possibility that I
    could develop warts on my genitals before the marriage?

3.	Is this possibility a reason for her or her family to refuse marriage to me?

4.	If I do develop warts during the marriage, how should my wife and I respond
    to this Islamically?

5.	Would it be a reason for her to divorce me if I do develop them, even though
    they can be easily treated every time they show? And they would not affect
    my ability to please her sexually.

6.	Should I be worried about this, given that warts are relatively harmless and
    the chances of me carrying them are low? May Allah forgive me for what I
    have done.

\ANSWER

In the Name of Allah, Most Gracious, Most Merciful.
All praise and thanks are due to Allah, and peace and blessings be upon His Messenger.

May Allah the Almighty accept your repentance and forgive you for your sin.
_Āmīn_.

1.	Islam does allow you to get married in the future regardless of having the
    possibility of developing warts. There is no harm in getting married with
    warts if your partner does not mind at all.

2.	Ethically speaking, you must tell your future wife about it in order for her
    to accept or reject. A Muslim has to be honest and just to others,
    especially his wife and kids.

3.	It is up to her or her family to accept you with this potential sickness.
    They have the right to go ahead with the marriage or to turn you down. You
    also have the same right to stop the process if you do not wish to continue

    medication and treatment through all possible sources and means. The Prophet
    \hon{\PBUH}{\BODY} said: “Use medical treatments, for Allah—the Mighty and
    Majestic—did not create any illness except that He created a cure for
    it—except for one ailment: old age.” Accordingly, Muslims must look for
    treatment for their sickness.

5.	It is her right to seek divorce if you develop warts and she does not want
    to stay with you. This right is granted by Islamic law to both of you. Yet,
    both of you should be just and fair to each other. Of course, if she seeks
    divorce on this ground, then she will have to pay back the dowry (_mahr_)
    you have given her. Her request will be considered _khulʿ_.

6.	You should not be too much worried about developing this sickness; rather,
    you have to seek treatment and medication through all means. Do not forget
    to pray to your Lord for His medication and forgiveness. He is in reality
    the only One who bestows health. I shall pray for your good health and
    protection. May Allah grant you cure and remedy.

Allah Almighty knows best.

## Conjugal relationships after the marriage contract but before the wedding celebration (_walīmah_)

\QUESTION{79}{10}{2}{Submitted by Habib from the United States on Jan 5, 2004}

_As-Salāmu ʿalaykum_. I had _katb al-kitāb_ with my cousin about a month ago. Thus,
Islamically we are married, but we are not going to have our wedding feast until
next year. Is it permissible if we sleep together? We have already done so, and
we enjoyed ourselves, but we didn’t have real intercourse because we are waiting
until our wedding night. Could you please advise me?

\ANSWER

_Wa ʿalaykum as-Salām wa raḥmatullāhi wa barakātuh_.

In the Name of Allah, Most Gracious, Most Merciful.
All praise and thanks are due to Allah, and peace and blessings be upon His Messenger.

May Allah guide you and your cousin. If you mean by _katb al-kitāb_ an
engagement (_khiṭbah_), I should inform you that _khiṭbah_ is just a promise to
get married, and hence, it is not a marriage; therefore, your relationship with
your cousin still remains the same as before the marriage.

In other words, you are not allowed to be with her without the presence of an
unmarriageable male (_maḥram_). Thus, she is a stranger to you until the
marriage contract is done. Therefore, you and your beloved cousin should stop
any type of contact leading to sinful relations such as sleeping or playing
together. It is not allowed at all.

Having said this, I call upon you and your cousin to have a marriage contract on
the spot. As for the wedding celebration, you have the right to postpone it to a
time of your convenience. May Allah Almighty grant both of you strong _īmān_.

On the other hand, if you mean by _katb al-kitāb_ that you have contracted the
marriage but did not yet have the wedding celebration, then there is no harm in
having the said relations. Thus, you are considered a married couple, and Islam
recommends that married couples have nice and lovely relations all the time as
stated in the Qurʾan: “And one of His signs is that He created for you spouses
from among yourselves so that you may find comfort in them, and He has created
love and kindness between you. Surely in this are signs for people who reflect.”
(Al-Rūm, 30:21)

As stated early, there is no harm, in this case, to sleep together. The wedding
feast is not a pre-condition, but it is strongly recommended by the Prophet
\hon{\PBUH}{\BODY}.

Allah Almighty knows best.
