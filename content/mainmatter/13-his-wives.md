
# His Wives

The first of his wives PBUH was Khadijah bint Khuwaylid ibn Asad ibn Abd al-Uzza
ibn Qusayy ibn Kilab. He PBUH married her when he was 25 years old. She passed
away RADF three years before the Hijrah. He didn't marry anyone else until she
passed away. Before him, she was married to Atiq ibn Abid ibn Abdillah ibn Umar
ibn Makhzum. She birthed Abdullah from him. After him, she was married to Abu
Haalah, whose name was Hind ibn Zuraarah ibn al-Nabbash ibn Adi ibn Habeeb ibn
S.urad ibn Salaamah ibn Jarwah ibn Usayyid ibn Amr ibn Tameem. She birthed two
sons for him: Hind and al-H.aarith, and a daughter named Zaynab. As for Hind ibn
Hind, he participated in Uhud, and lived in Basrah. Al-Hasan ibn Ali ibn Abi
Talib narrated [ahadith] from him. As for al-Harith, one of the Kuffār killed
him at the Rukn al-Yamaani.

When Khadijah passed away, he PBUH married Sawdah bint Zama3ah ibn Qays ibn Abd
al-Shams ibn Abd Wudd ibn Nasr ibn Malik ibn Hisl ibn Aamir ibn Lu'ayy. Before
him, she was married to her paternal uncle's son, al-Sakraan ibn Amr ibn Abd
Shams; he died leaving her.

After her, the Messenger of Allah PBUH married Aisha bint Abi Bakr al-Siddiq,
whose name was Abdullah ibn Abi Quhafah -- whose name was Uthman -- ibn Aamir
ibn Amr ibn Ka'b ibn Sa3d ibn Taym ibn Murrah ibn Ka'b ibn Lu'ayy ibn Ghalib.

He didn't marry any virgin besides her. He married her in Makkah when she was
six years old and consummated the marriage seven months after the Hijrah, in
Shawwal; she was nine years old. She remained with him for nine years and five
months. She passed away in 58AH.

He then married Hafsah bint Umar ibn al-Khattab two years and a month after the
Hijrah. Before him, she was married to Khunays ibn Hudhaafah al-Sahmi; he passed
away leaving her. She passed away in 45AH. Marwan performed her janazah prayer;
he was the Ameer of Madinah.

He then married Zaynab bint Khuzaymah ibn al-Harith ibn Abdillah ibn Amr ibn
Abdillah ibn Abd Manaf ibn Hilal ibn Aamir ibn Sa3sa3ah. Before him, she was
married to Ubaydah ibn al-Harith ibn al-Mutallib ibn Abd Manaf. He was killed on
the day of [the Battle of] Badr. Zaynab passed away during his lifetime after
(spending?) two months with him. Al-Zuhri said, “Rather, she was previously
married to Abdullah ibn Jahsh al-Asadi, a martyr of the Day of Uhud.”

He also married Umm Salamah, whose name was Hind, daughter of Abu Umayyah, whose
name was Hudhayfah, son of al-Mughirah ibn Abdillah ibn Umar ibn Makhzum ibn
Yaqaz.ah ibn Murrah ibn Ka3b ibn Lu'ayy. She was previously married to Abu
Salamah, whose name was Abdullah, son of Abd al-Asad al-Makhzumi; she gave birth
to Umar, Salamah, Durrah, and Zaynab. She was the last of his wives to pass
away. She passed away in 59AH. This is how Abu Hassan al-Hasan ibn Uthman
al-Ziyaadi mentioned it in his Ta'rikh: that she passed away in the year 59AH.
Ibn Abi Khaythamah said, “A year before [the death of] Muawiyah.” Ataa' said,
“The last of them (Prophet's wives) to die was Safiyyah,” but this is a mistake
(wahm).

He married Zaynab, daughter of Jahsh ibn Rithaab ibn Ya'mur ibn S.abirah ibn
Murrah ibn Kabir ibn Ghanm ibn Duudaan ibn Asad ibn Khuzaymah. Before him PBUH,
she was married to Zayd ibn Harithah, the Prophet's freed slave. She was the
first of his wives to pass away after him PBUH. She died in the beginning of the
Khilafah of Umar. She was whom Allah the Exalted married to him. After the
conquests, when Umar gave her what was allotted to her, she cried and أعولت. She
supplicated to Allah that the following year not be shown to her until she met
the Messenger of Allah على ما فارقته من التقلل في الدنيا. She died before the
end of that very year.

He then married Juwayriyah bint al-Ḥārith ibn Abī Ḍirār -- whose name was Ḥabīb
-- ibn al-Ḥārith ibn ʿĀbid ibn Mālik ibn Jadhīmah, who was al-Musṭaliq from [the
tribe of] Khuzāʿah. Before him, she was married to a man from her paternal
uncle's sons whose name was ʿAbdullāh ibn Jaḥash al-Asadī. She passed away in 56
AH, in Rabīʿ al-Awwal. Marwān prayed her _janāzah_. This was mentioned by
al-Wāqidī.

He then married Umm Ḥabībah, whose name was Ramlah. It was also said that it was
Hind. She was the daughter of Abū Sufyān Ṣakhr ibn Ḥarb ibn Umayyah ibn ʿAbd
Shams briefly after [the Treaty of] al-Ḥudaybīyah.

سيقت إليه من بلاد الحبشة، وكانت هنالك مهاجرة مُسلمة

Before him, she was married to ʿUbaydullāh ibn Jaḥsh al-Asadī, who apostatized
to Christianity.

It was said that al-Najāshī أصدقها  400 dinars of gold. She passed away during
the Khilāfah of her brother Muʿāwiyah in 44 AH, according to what Abū Ḥassān
said. Similar was said by al-Wāqidī.

Briefly after the conquest of Khaybar, he married Ṣafīyah bint Ḥuyayy ibn
Akhṭab, from Banū al-Naḍīr. She was from the descendants of the Messenger of
Allāh, Hārūn ibn ʿImrān, who was the brother of Mūsá ibn ʿImrān
\hon{S_TWO}{\BODY}. Their father was ʿImrān ibn Qāhaāth ibn Lāwī who was the son
of the Messenger of Allāh, Yaʿqūb who was the son of the Messenger of Allāh,
Isḥāq, who was the son of the Messenger of Allāh, Ibrāhīm, His Messenger and
Khalīl. Before him, she was married to Kinānah ibn Abī al-Ḥuqayq. Al-Wāqidī
said, “In the year 50, Ṣafīyah bint Ḥuyayy passed away.” The same was said by
Abū Ḥassān al-Ziyādī.

He then married Maymūnah bint al-Ḥārith ibn Ḥazn ibn Bajīr ibn Haram ibn
Ruwaybah ibn ʿAbdillāh ibn Hilāl ibn ʿĀmir ibn Ṣaʿṣaʿah. She was the maternal
aunt of Khālid ibn al-Walīd and ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿAbbās. Before the Messenger of
Allāh, she was married to Abū Ruhm ibn ʿAbd al-ʿUzzá ibn Abī Qays ibn ʿAbd Wadd
ibn Naṣr ibn Mālik ibn Ḥisl ibn ʿĀmir ibn Luʾayy. ʿAbdullāh ibn Muḥammad ibn
ʿAqīl ibn Abī Ṭālib said, “Rather, she was previously married to Ḥuwayṭib ibn
ʿAbd al-ʿUzzá, who was the brother of Abū Ruhm.”

She was the last whom he \hon{\PBUH}{\BODY} married. He married her in Makkah
during the Farewell Umrah after coming out of Iḥrām, and consumated the marriage
in Sarif, where she later passed away during the reign of Muʿāwiyah. This was in
51 AH. This was stated by Khalīfah. Her grave there is well-known.

It is not authentically established that he \hon{\PBUH}{\BODY} ever divorced a
woman, except for Ḥafṣah bint ʿUmar, whose divorce he later revoked due to Allāh
commanding him to revoke it.

[However,] he \hon{\PBUH}{\BODY} did want to divorce Sawdah bint Zamʿah when she
advanced in age, \c1{fearing} \c2{expecting} that he would not be able to do
right by her. But she was keen on him keeping her, and thus gave her day to
ʿĀʾishah bint Abī Bakr, and so he kept her.

There remained no one from his wives, the Mother of the Believers, any woman
except that she chose him when Allāh the Exalted revealed the Āyah of Choice
(_āyat al-takhyīr_). Whoever mentions otherwise has definitely mentioned
\c1{الباطل المُتَيَقِّن} \c2{clear falsehood}.

It is true that the _mahr_ he gave his wives was 500 dirhams each. This is what
is established, except for Ṣafīyah, whom he emancipated and made her
emancipation her _mahr_; she had no other _mahr_ besides this. This became the
\c1{Sunnah} after him \hon{\PBUH}{\BODY}.

He gave the wedding feast for Zaynab bint Jaḥsh with a single sheep and it
sufficed the people. Anas ibn Mālik said, “We never saw him give the wedding
feast for any of his wives with more than this.”

He gave the wedding feast for Ṣafīyah which had no \c1{شحم} nor meat. It
consisted of \c1{السويق}, \c1{التّمر}, and \c1{السَّمن}.

He gave the wedding feast for some of his wives -- without naming them -- with
two _mudd_ of \c1{شعير} and it was sufficient for every one who attended.

Every year, he would spent on each of his wives 20 _wasq_ of \c1{شعير}, 80
_wasq_ of dates. This is what we have narrated through a route of the highest
authenticity, as well as through a route which has some weakness: that this
amount was for every single one of them in a year, and Allāh knows best. And
each one of them had bondwomen, bondservants, and freed slaves, while he was alive.
